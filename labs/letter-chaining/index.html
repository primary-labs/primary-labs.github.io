    Record the letter sounds

    Say just the sound. Each recording stops on its own after 4 seconds. Until you record a sound, kids hear the page's own voice.

    Record the words

    Say each word clearly, once. Until you record a word, kids hear the page's own voice. New words from your chains use the device's built-in voice instead, so record those for the best sound.

    The page's own voice is computer-generated (Kokoro, an open-source voice model). It was made ahead of time and is part of the page, so no AI runs while kids use it. Your recordings always play instead when you make them.

    Make your own chain

    Type 2- or 3-letter words in order, using any letters. Each word changes exactly one letter, and every word in a chain is the same length. Every word gets a picture. Made-up words like "zog" get their own creature.

      Custom chains are saved in this browser only.

      Standards

      NYS Next Generation English Language Arts Learning Standards: Grade 1, Reading Foundations (RF2: Demonstrate understanding of spoken words, syllables, and sounds).

      1RF2b

      Orally produce single-syllable words by blending sounds (phonemes), including consonant blends.

      Where: Blend it in Letter Chains. Each sound lights up and plays, and kids say it too. Then the arrow sweeps and they say the whole word fast. After every new word, the page blends it once more.

      1RF2c

      Isolate and pronounce initial, medial vowel, and final sounds (phonemes) in spoken single-syllable words.

      Where: Every link in a chain asks, "Which sound changed: beginning, middle, or end?" Kids tap that sound's tile. Sound Spy practices finding a sound's position. Hint lights up the position that changes.

      A 5-minute chaining routine

      1. Say it. Play the new word (or say it yourself). Kids repeat it.
      2. Find it. Ask: "Which sound changed: beginning, middle, or end?" Use the traffic lights: green, yellow, red.
      3. Swap it. A student taps that tile, then taps the new letter.
      4. Blend it. Press Blend it. Say each sound as it lights up, then say it fast.
      5. Use it. Read the sentence. Ask a student to make their own sentence.

      Tips

      • Vowel tiles are red and consonant tiles are blue. Every built-in word needs a red tile in the middle.
      • Keep stop sounds short (/t/, /d/, /k/, /g/). Stretch /nnn/.
      • Short o is the hardest vowel for many first graders. The "Short o Trail" chain keeps the vowel the same, so kids can focus on the beginning and end sounds.
      • On a projector or interactive board, students can also type letters on the keyboard. Arrow keys pick a tile.
