Sound Boxes

    For teachers

    How to use it, standards and tips

    Practice for after a lesson on blending and segmenting, not a replacement for it. Each word has three sounds and three letters, like cat or sun. There are two activities.

    How to use it

    1. Blend. The student hears the word's sounds one at a time, and each box lights up as its sound plays. They tap a box to hear its sound again, say the word, then tap its picture. The two other pictures are one sound away (for cat: hat and can), so every sound matters. A right picture says the word and shows its letters.
    2. Segment, say it. The student hears a word and sees its picture. They say each sound out loud as they tap or slide a counter into each box. Each box plays its sound as the counter lands, so they can check themselves.
    3. Segment, spell it. They put letters in the boxes. A wrong letter says its own sound and stays where it is, and the box says the sound it needs.

    Standards

    NYS Next Generation English Language Arts Learning Standards, Kindergarten and Grade 1.

    KRF2d

    Blend and segment individual sounds (phonemes) in spoken one-syllable words.

    Where: Blend joins three sounds into a word. Segment's counters break a word into its three sounds.

    KRF3a

    Demonstrate one-to-one letter-sound correspondence by producing the primary sound or most frequent sound for each consonant.

    Where: In Segment's letter step, students match each consonant sound to its letter, and every letter says its sound when it is tapped.

    KRF3b

    Decode short vowel sounds with common spellings.

    Where: Every word has a short vowel, and each level practices one vowel. The letter tray always has a second vowel to choose from.

    1RF3c

    Decode regularly spelled one-syllable words.

    Where: Students link each sound of a regularly spelled word to its letter, then read the finished word in the boxes.

    Grade 1 students also count, blend and segment sounds as in 1RF2a, but these words have no consonant blends (like st or fl), so the lab doesn't cover that standard.

    Tips

    • Ask students to say every sound out loud. Saying the sounds is the practice; the taps only check it.
    • Pick a level with the Levels button at the top. Short a, i, o, u and e each practice one vowel. Mixed reviews all five.
    • A level always has the same words in the same order. To share one, open it and copy the page address. It ends with the level's name, like #short-a.
    • Tap a box at any time to hear its sound. Tap the picture, or the yellow button, to hear the word or the sounds again.
    • There is no timer and no score. A wrong answer just plays the sounds again.
    • The bell at the top turns sound effects off. The voice stays on.
    • The voice is a computer voice. Nothing students do is sent anywhere.
