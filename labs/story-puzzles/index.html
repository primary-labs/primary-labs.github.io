    For teachers

    How to use it, standards and tips

    Each short decodable story comes with six picture pieces. Four show details from the story. The other two each differ from a story word by one sound, like map for mat or pot for pit, so kids have to read every letter.

    How to use it

    1. Read. The student reads the story, out loud to you or to themselves.
    2. Find. They tap or drag a piece that is in the story. It moves to its spot under the story, in story order, and its sentence lights up and is read aloud.
    3. Check. A piece that isn't in the story fades, and the voice says, "That one is not in the story. Read it again."
    4. Retell. When all four pieces are in, they show the story in order. Ask the student to retell the story with the pieces.

    Standards

    NYS Next Generation English Language Arts Learning Standards, Grade 1.

    1RF3c

    Decode regularly spelled one-syllable words.

    Where: To tell mop from map, kids have to sound out each word.

    1RF4

    Read beginning reader texts, appropriate to individual student ability, with sufficient accuracy and fluency to support comprehension.

    Where: Kids read a short decodable story, then show they understood it by finding the pieces that match.

    1R7

    Use illustrations and details in literary and informational texts to discuss story elements and/or topics. (RI&RL)

    Where: Kids match each piece to a detail in the story. Ask what each piece shows and where the story says it.

    1R1

    Develop and answer questions about key ideas and details in a text. (RI&RL)

    Where: For each piece, kids answer the question "Is this in the story?" from what they read.

    Kindergarten students reading at this level practice KRF3b (decode short vowel sounds with common spellings) and KR7 (describe the relationship between illustrations and the text).

    Tips

    • Have students read first, then tap Hear the story to check.
    • Tap any sentence to hear it. Tap a placed piece to hear its sentence again, or an empty spot to hear the directions.
    • Pick a story with the Stories button at the top. They're grouped by short vowel, so you can match what you've taught. At the Pond mixes all five vowels for review.
    • To share one story, open it and copy the page address. It ends with the story's name, like #fat-cat.
    • The stories also use these high-frequency words: a, the, is, has, to, they, have, see, go, was, puts. Pre-teach any your students don't know yet.
    • The bell at the top turns sound effects off. The voice stays on.
    • The voice is a computer voice. Nothing kids do is sent anywhere.
